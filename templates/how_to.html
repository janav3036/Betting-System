How to Play

Everything you need to know to get started on BetBoard.

1

Getting Started

Register with your roll number and a username. Your roll number must be in the class directory — unrecognised numbers are blocked.

Every new account starts with 1,000 coins. Coins are your currency for placing bets.

2

Standard Events

Most events are YES / NO questions. Click any open event card on the dashboard to open it.

Enter how many coins you want to bet, pick a side, and submit. You can only bet once per event, so choose carefully.

When the admin resolves the event, winners are paid out based on the odds locked in at the time of their bet.

3

How Odds Work

Odds are live and parimutuel — they shift as more people bet. The more coins on one side, the lower that side's payout.

Example
YES pool: 1,000 coins
YES odds: 1.5×
NO pool: 500 coins
NO odds: 3.0×

Your payout if you win = coins bet × odds at time of your bet. Odds lock in the moment you submit.

4

Most Likely To (MLT) Events

MLT events run in two phases:

  1. Nomination — submit one anonymous vote for whoever you think fits the question. Search by name or roll number.
  2. Betting — the top 5 nominees are revealed. Bet on who you think the admin will pick as the winner.

Odds per nominee work the same way — the more bets a nominee gets, the lower the payout.

5

Leaderboard

There are three leaderboard tabs:

  • Weekly — total profit from resolved bets in the last 7 days.
  • All-Time — current coin balance ranking across all users.
  • Group — groups ranked by total coins held by their members.

Click any username on the All-Time tab to view their public profile and stats.

6

Groups

Groups let you compete with a specific set of classmates.

  • Create a group and invite others by roll number.
  • Invited users who haven't registered yet will be auto-added when they sign up.
  • Group P&L tracks how much each member has gained or lost since joining.
  • Groups are capped at 10 members.
Tip Watch the odds before betting. If everyone is rushing to one side, the other side may offer a much better payout — if you think they're wrong.
